# Barak Rosenshine’s 10 Principles of Instruction: Direct Cognitive Scaffolding

## 0. Capability Contract

After studying this master module, you will be able to:
* **Dismantle and reconstruct** any passive, lecture-heavy lesson plan into a high-fidelity Rosenshine instructional cycle ($I\ Do \rightarrow We\ Do \rightarrow You\ Do$).
* **Execute** formative Checks for Understanding (CFU) that sample 100% of learners concurrently, eliminating the "illusion of comprehension" caused by calling on volunteer hands.
* **Enforce** the empirical **80% Success Rate Gate** during guided practice before releasing learners to independent work.
* **Configure** an AI conversational tutor to guide learners through explicit sub-goal modeling, faded scaffolds, and spaced cumulative retrieval.

> **Pre-reading Recognition Challenge**:
> Three secondary teachers are teaching quadratic factoring ($x^2 + 5x + 6$). Which teacher is executing genuine Rosenshine instruction?
> * **Teacher 1**: Explains the mathematical formula on the board for 35 minutes, asks *"Does everyone understand?"*, waits 3 seconds, and assigns exercises 1–25 from the textbook for homework.
> * **Teacher 2**: Puts students into groups of four with algebra tiles and tells them to *"explore the tiles to discover how rectangles relate to polynomials"*.
> * **Teacher 3**: Conducts a 4-minute retrieval warmup on factors of 6, models one problem thinking out loud, leads the class through two isomorphic problems where every student writes the intermediate step on a mini-whiteboard, and only assigns independent practice once 28 out of 30 whiteboards show correct work.
> 
> *Answer*: Only **Teacher 3** embodies Rosenshine. Teachers 1 and 2 represent the two classic failure modes: the *Lecture-Homework Illusion* and *Unguided Discovery Overload*.

## 1. The Instructional Problem Solved

Traditional instructional delivery suffers from two catastrophic design flaws:
1. **The Curse of Knowledge & Expert Blindness**: The teacher's highly automated long-term schemas lead them to skip intermediate steps, presenting novel concepts in chunks that are far too large for novice working memory.
2. **The "Volunteer Hands" Mirage**: Teachers ask an open question (*"Who can tell me the next step?"*), call on one of the three confident students waving their hands, receive a correct answer, and mistakenly conclude that the entire class of 30 understands.

Rosenshine’s synthesis (derived from cognitive laboratory science, observations of master teachers with the highest standardized gains, and randomized classroom trials) solves these flaws by transforming instruction into an interactive, step-by-step cognitive apprenticeship.

## 2. The 4 Operational Clusters of Rosenshine

Rather than treating the 10 principles as an isolated checklist, master educators group them into 4 functional phases:

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ PHASE 1: REACTIVATION (Principles 1 & 10)                   │
│ Daily Review (5-8 min) ── Spaced Weekly & Monthly Retrieval │
└─────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────┘
                              ▼
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ PHASE 2: "I DO" — MODELING (Principles 2, 4, 8)            │
│ Small Steps ── Worked Examples with Think-Aloud ── Scaffolds│
└─────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────┘
                              ▼
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ PHASE 3: "WE DO" — GUIDED PRACTICE (Principles 3, 5, 6, 7)  │
│ High Frequency Questions ── 100% Sampling CFU ── 80% Gate   │
└─────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────┘
                              ▼
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ PHASE 4: "YOU DO" — INDEPENDENT PRACTICE (Principle 9)      │
│ Fluent Automation ── Unassisted Problem Solving ── Transfer │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## 3. The 10 Principles Deconstructed

| Principle | Core Cognitive Mechanism | Master Implementation Move | Superficial Failure Mode (Anti-Pattern) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Daily Review** | Retrieval Practice & Spacing Effect; lowers working memory friction for the day's new lesson. | 5-minute low-stakes retrieval grid covering content from yesterday, last week, and last month. | Asking: *"What did we talk about yesterday?"* and letting one student answer while the rest daydream. |
| **2. Small Steps** | Working Memory bandwidth ($4 \pm 1$ chunks); prevents cognitive overload. | Break complex procedures into 2–3 sub-goals; practice each sub-goal before chaining them together. | Delivering a 45-minute continuous PowerPoint lecture containing 14 new abstract concepts. |
| **3. Ask Questions** | Active processing (ICAP framework); forces retrieval and exposes faulty mental models. | Ask a question $\rightarrow$ Wait 3–5 seconds $\rightarrow$ Demand non-verbal response from all students. | Calling on the first student who raises their hand without providing wait time. |
| **4. Provide Models** | Worked Example Effect; gives novices an external cognitive schema. | Live-model problem solving while vocalizing internal doubts, decisions, and checks (Think-Aloud). | Showing an already-completed slide with all 10 lines of math visible simultaneously. |
| **5. Guide Practice** | Rehearsal in working memory under teacher supervision; prevents encoding misconceptions. | Allocate 50%+ of lesson time to guided practice with corrective feedback. | Jumping directly from teacher explanation to silent independent seatwork. |
| **6. Check for Understanding** | Real-time diagnostic signal detection. | Use mini-whiteboards, finger signals (1-4), or digital response polls to see 100% of responses. | Asking: *"Does this make sense? Any questions?"* and assuming silence equals mastery. |
| **7. High Success Rate** | Prevents frustration and reinforcement of errors; establishes self-efficacy. | Require $\ge 80\%$ class accuracy on guided practice before moving to independent practice. | Releasing students to independent seatwork when 40% of the class is still struggling. |
| **8. Provide Scaffolds** | Temporary cognitive support structures in the Zone of Proximal Development. | Use graphic organizers, sentence starters, or worked-example fading; explicitly plan when to remove them. | Providing permanent crutches that are never faded, preventing independent capability. |
| **9. Independent Practice** | Overlearning & Schema Automation; frees working memory for future complex tasks. | Extensive, uninterrupted problem sets where students apply the automated schema. | Allowing students to constantly ask the teacher for help during independent practice without trying first. |
| **10. Spaced Review** | Consolidation into long-term memory; battles the Ebbinghaus Forgetting Curve. | Dedicate 15 minutes every Friday and 30 minutes monthly to cumulative spaced retrieval. | "Cram-and-dump" chapter testing where topics are never revisited once assessed. |

## 4. Fully Worked Classroom Execution Transcript

### Context: Grade 8 Algebra — Solving Two-Step Equations ($3x + 7 = 22$)

#### Phase 1: Daily Review (4 Minutes)
* **Teacher**: *"Pencils down, eyes on the screen. On your mini-whiteboards, solve these two warmup items from last week: (1) $20 - 7$, (2) $15 \div 3$. You have 30 seconds. 3, 2, 1... Chin it!"*
* *(All 28 students hold up boards. Teacher scans in 3 seconds: 100% correct).*
* **Teacher**: *"Excellent. Working memory cleared and ready."*

#### Phase 2: "I Do" — Modeling with Think-Aloud (5 Minutes)
* **Teacher**: *"Look at equation 1: $3x + 7 = 22$. Watch what my brain does. My goal is to isolate $x$. Right now, $x$ is being multiplied by 3, and then 7 is added. I must undo operations in reverse order. What was the last thing done to $x$? Adding 7. What is the inverse of adding 7? Subtracting 7. So I subtract 7 from BOTH sides to keep the balance. $22 - 7 = 15$. Now I have $3x = 15$. What is holding $x$? Multiplication by 3. Inverse? Divide by 3. $x = 5$. Now my critical check: $3(5) + 7 = 15 + 7 = 22$. It matches."*

#### Phase 3: "We Do" — Guided Practice & 80% Success Gate (12 Minutes)
* **Teacher**: *"Problem 2 on your boards: $4x + 9 = 33$. Step 1 only: Write the inverse operation you must perform to both sides. Do not solve yet. 15 seconds... Boards up!"*
* *(Teacher scans: 27 boards show '$- 9$', 1 board shows '$+ 9$').*
* **Teacher (Immediate Corrective Feedback)**: *"Eyes on me. Marcus, look at the sign in front of 9. It is $+9$. What undoes addition? Subtraction. Fix it on your board... Perfect. Now everyone, perform the subtraction and write the new line. 20 seconds... Boards up!"*
* *(28/28 boards show $4x = 24$. Class repeats for final division step. 93% success rate achieved).*

#### Phase 4: "You Do" — Independent Practice (15 Minutes)
* **Teacher**: *"You have proven you own the schema. Turn to page 84, complete problems 1 through 8 independently in your notebooks. No talking for the first 10 minutes."*

## 5. Non-Example: The "I Did, Now You Do" Collapse

**The Anti-Pattern**:
The teacher works through 3 complex examples on the board while students watch passively. The teacher asks: *"Is everyone following?"* Two students nod. The teacher announces: *"Great, do the worksheet."*

*Why this collapses*:
* The critical **"We Do" (Guided Practice)** phase was completely skipped.
* Novices experienced the *Illusion of Competence* while watching the expert, but because they never held the intermediate representations in their own working memory, their schemas were never encoded.
* Within 3 minutes of seatwork, 15 students raise their hands asking: *"I don't get it, what do I do first?"*

## 6. Formative Hinge Question & Diagnostic Map

### The Hinge Diagnostic Item
A mentor teacher is observing an instructional coach working with an elementary science teacher on Rosenshine's Principle 6 (Check for Understanding). Which teacher behavior demonstrates a failure to understand the core purpose of a CFU?

* **A)** Asking students to indicate true/false by showing thumbs-up or thumbs-down against their chests simultaneously.
* **B)** Asking: *"Can anyone tell me why the plant in the dark died?"* and calling on Maya, who has her hand raised first.
* **C)** Giving students 60 seconds to write a one-sentence summary on an index card and collecting all cards before proceeding.
* **D)** Displaying a multiple-choice question with 4 common misconceptions and having every student hold up 1, 2, 3, or 4 fingers on the count of three.

## 7. Turn-Key Implementation Checklist: The Daily Rosenshine Audit

Before teaching any lesson or programming an AI tutor persona, verify these 5 checkpoints:

- [ ] **1. Daily Review**: Did I open with a 3–5 minute retrieval task covering prior prerequisites without looking at notes?
- [ ] **2. Chunk Size**: Is my new direct instruction segment under 10 minutes before requiring student physical/verbal production?
- [ ] **3. Think-Aloud Modeling**: Did I vocalize *why* I made each choice, including how to spot and avoid a common pitfall?
- [ ] **4. 100% Sampling CFU**: Did I require a simultaneous response from *every* learner (not just volunteer hands) before releasing them?
- [ ] **5. 80% Success Gate**: Did at least 8 out of 10 students demonstrate procedural accuracy during guided practice before independent work began?

## 8. AI Tutor Execution State Machine

```
[ STATE 0: WARMUP ]
  Prompt: Ask 2 retrieval questions on prior session concepts.
  Check: If accuracy < 100%, supply immediate feedback before new topic.

[ STATE 1: SMALL STEPS & MODELING ]
  Tutor Action: Present sub-goal 1 + worked example with explicit reasoning.
  Constraint: Maximum 120 words of text. Never dump full solution at once.

[ STATE 2: GUIDED PRACTICE ("WE DO") ]
  Tutor Action: Present paired problem with step 1 pre-filled.
  Prompt: "Complete step 2 only. What is your calculation?"
  Branch:
    - If Correct: Transition to problem without scaffolding.
    - If Incorrect: Diagnose misconception, give clue (not answer), re-prompt.

[ STATE 3: INDEPENDENT CHECK ("YOU DO") ]
  Tutor Action: Present novel isomorphic problem. No scaffolding provided.
  Gate: If student succeeds, advance difficulty. If student fails, return to State 1.
```

# Dual Coding Theory & Multimedia Learning

![Paivio Dual Coding Convergent Optical Prism](../../assets/dual_coding_prism_1789440523520.jpg)

![Paivio Dual Coding Convergent Optical Prism](../../assets/dual_coding_prism_1789440523520.jpg)

> [!NOTE]  
> Learning improves when instruction helps learners construct and coordinate useful verbal and pictorial representations without exceeding limited processing capacity. The causal mechanism is **active selection, organization, and integration**, not the mere presence of two media and not a presumed learner identity such as “visual learner.”

---

## 1\. Learning Contract

- **Prerequisites**: The educator should understand that working memory is capacity-limited, that prior knowledge changes effective cognitive load, and that learning requires building or revising schemas rather than merely exposing learners to information.
- **Observable Competencies**: By the end of this module, the educator can:
  
  1. **Diagnose** whether a multimedia lesson reduces representational ambiguity or merely adds competing information.
  2. **Design** coordinated verbal and pictorial representations whose relationships are perceptually and conceptually explicit.
  3. **Scaffold** novice learners in selecting, organizing, and connecting representations before progressively withdrawing guidance.
  4. **Critique** multimedia materials for redundancy, seductive detail, split attention, transient information, inaccurate simplification, learner-control problems, and false claims about “learning styles.”
- **Diagnostic Prediction**: An educator who understands the mechanism will sometimes deliberately remove an image, animation, caption, narration track, or decorative element. They will predict learning from the **processing demanded by the representation**, rather than from media count, visual attractiveness, engagement ratings, or student modality preference.
- **Epistemic contract**: A learner who can follow an AI-generated diagram has demonstrated assisted performance, not necessarily independent competence. AI may reduce search, drawing, explanation, or comparison costs, but the learner must retain responsibility for deciding whether the representation is accurate and for reconstructing the causal model without hidden assistance.

A teacher has mastered this module only when they can explain why the same diagram may substantially help one lesson, contribute almost nothing to another, and actively impair a third.

## 2\. The Intuitive Dilemma (Why Naive Teaching Fails)

Consider a secondary biology teacher preparing a lesson on blood circulation.

The teacher has learned the slogan “people learn better from words and pictures than from words alone.” They therefore redesign a slide.

The original contains six sentences describing blood flow.

The improved slide contains:

- those same six sentences;
- a detailed anatomical heart;
- animated arrows;
- a photograph of a surgeon;
- labels for twenty-two structures;
- an animated heartbeat;
- narration reading the six sentences verbatim;
- subtitles showing those same sentences;
- background music.

Students describe the slide as “clear,” “professional,” and “interesting.”

During questioning, however, several students cannot answer:

> “If the pulmonary artery became blocked, which part of the circuit would be affected immediately afterward, and why?”

The teacher is surprised because students saw the process in several forms.

The intuitive model was:

> More representations → more encoding → stronger memory.

The missing variable is **what the learner had to do cognitively**.

One student was watching the arrows.

Another was trying to match twenty-two labels to unfamiliar structures.

Another was reading subtitles while hearing identical narration.

Another attended to the surgeon photograph.

Several remembered that the heart has four chambers but failed to construct the causal circuit connecting chamber, vessel, lung, oxygenation, and systemic return.

The lesson contained more information but afforded less coherent processing.

### The teacher's initial diagnosis

**Teacher:** “They need to see it again.”

**Instructional coach:** “What specifically did they fail to understand?”

**Teacher:** “The path.”

**Coach:** “Then why are twenty-two labels, a photograph, subtitles, and background music competing for the same lesson goal?”

**Teacher:** “I thought using several channels helped.”

**Coach:** “Several channels can help. Several competing tasks do not.”

The distinction is foundational.

A useful multimedia representation allows the learner to coordinate complementary information:

> “The narration says pressure closes the inlet valve while the diagram shows where the valve moves.”

A pathological representation makes the learner allocate resources among unnecessary sources:

> “Read this sentence, hear the same sentence, inspect an animation, decode twenty labels, and determine which parts correspond.”

The multimedia principle is therefore not:

> Add pictures.

It is closer to:

> **Represent relationships in forms that permit useful complementary processing, then help the learner coordinate those representations.**

This interpretation aligns with the broader empirical record. Guo, Zhang, Wright, and McTigue's 2020 meta-analysis included **39 experimental studies and N = 2,103 learners** and estimated a moderate overall advantage for adding graphics to text, **Hedges' g = 0.39, 95% CI [0.26, 0.51]**. The individual effects ranged from negative to strongly positive, immediately falsifying the proposition that adding any graphic reliably improves comprehension. [![Generated Image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://journals.sagepub.com&sz=128) Sage Journals+1](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/2332858420901696?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

That variation is pedagogically more important than the slogan.

## 3\. Active Prediction Challenge

A teacher must explain how a bicycle pump works to mechanically inexperienced students.

The target outcome is **transfer**: students must predict what happens if a valve sticks rather than merely recall component names.

Choose the design most likely to support that goal.

**A.** Narrate the mechanism first, then show the complete animation.

**B.** Play the animation while simultaneously narrating the corresponding causal steps.

**C.** Play the animation while displaying the complete narration as written text and reading it aloud.

**D.** Show a realistic high-resolution pump animation with no explanation so students can infer the mechanism themselves.

Commit to one answer before continuing.

The best prediction is **B**, but the reason matters more than the answer.

Mayer and Anderson's classic experiments tested essentially this temporal-coordination problem. Their 1991 studies involved **N = 102 mechanically inexperienced college students**. Transfer advantages for synchronized rather than separated representations were approximately **d = 1.00 and d = 1.05** in the two experiments. [![Generated Image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://eric.ed.gov&sz=128) ERIC+1](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ442338&utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Their 1992 follow-up involved **N = 280 students** studying pump and braking mechanisms. Corresponding temporal-contiguity effects on transfer were approximately **d = 1.61 and d = 1.33**. [![Generated Image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://eric.ed.gov&sz=128) ERIC+1](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ456638&utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The interpretation is not “animation is powerful.”

Animation alone was insufficient.

Narration alone was insufficient for the strongest transfer.

The advantage emerged when learners could construct and connect corresponding verbal and pictorial representations while the referents remained cognitively available.

## 4\. The Underlying Cognitive Mechanism

### 4.1 Dual Coding Theory: two representational systems

Paivio's Dual Coding Theory proposes partly distinct but interconnected systems for representing verbal and nonverbal information.

A learner encountering:

> “The piston moving upward reduces pressure inside the cylinder”

can construct a verbal representation.

If the learner simultaneously sees a schematic showing:

- piston rising;
- chamber volume increasing;
- inlet valve opening;
- air entering;

they can also construct a nonverbal representation.

Learning may benefit when connections form:

```
VERBAL MODEL
"piston rises"
      ↕
"volume increases"
      ↕
"pressure decreases"

        ⇅ referential connections

PICTORIAL MODEL
[piston ↑]
[chamber expands]
[valve opens]
[air enters]
```

This does **not** mean that memory contains a literal verbal box and visual box with perfectly independent capacities.

Dual Coding Theory is a representational theory, not a license for simplistic neuroscience.

### 4.2 Cognitive Theory of Multimedia Learning

Mayer's Cognitive Theory of Multimedia Learning extends the instructional account around three broad assumptions:

1. humans process visually/pictorially represented and auditory/verbal information through partly differentiated processing systems;
2. processing capacity is limited;
3. meaningful learning requires active cognitive processing.

The learner must perform at least three classes of operation:

```
INPUT
  ↓
SELECT
relevant words and images
  ↓
ORGANIZE
verbal model + pictorial model
  ↓
INTEGRATE
words ↔ pictures ↔ prior knowledge
  ↓
MENTAL MODEL
capable of explanation and transfer
```

A picture does not automatically enter a learner's mental model.

Neither does a sentence.

Both must be selected and interpreted.

### 4.3 Why coordination matters

Imagine this sequence:

**Narration at minute 0:30**

> “When the handle rises, the piston creates increased volume below it.”

**Relevant image at minute 2:10**

The learner must retain the earlier proposition while searching the later animation for its referent.

The instruction has converted a perceptual matching problem into a memory problem.

Now compare simultaneous presentation:

**Teacher:** “Watch this chamber.”

The teacher points.

**Teacher:** “As the piston rises, what happens to its volume?”

**Student:** “It gets bigger.”

**Teacher:** “And pressure?”

**Student:** “Lower.”

The representation and proposition are available together. Integration becomes cheaper.

That logic motivates **spatial contiguity** and **temporal contiguity**.

### 4.4 Why redundant media can hurt

A common misreading is:

> If verbal plus visual is good, verbal plus visual plus more verbal must be better.

Consider:

```
VISUAL PROCESSING DEMANDS

animation ───────────────┐
                         ├── competing visual attention
identical subtitles ─────┘

AUDITORY PROCESSING
narration
```

When the same explanatory language is spoken and simultaneously printed beside a visually complex animation, learners may repeatedly switch their eyes between text and diagram.

Mayer, Heiser, and Lonn's 2001 experiments found that adding concurrent on-screen text to narrated animation could reduce retention and transfer, and that interesting but irrelevant additions could also impair learning. [![Generated Image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://www.csus.edu&sz=128) Sacramento State+1](https://www.csus.edu/cpns/epperson/_internal/_documents/courses/hist107/Mayer_Heiser_Lonn_2001.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

This does not establish a universal “never use captions” rule.

Captions remain essential for accessibility and can be useful when:

- narration is difficult to hear;
- terminology is unfamiliar;
- learners study in a second language;
- the visual display itself is simple;
- learners can pause;
- selected key text rather than verbatim duplication is displayed.

Design requires reasoning about actual processing demands.

### 4.5 Modality is conditional, not magical

Ginns's 2005 meta-analysis synthesized **43 independent effects** and reported an overall modality effect of **d = 0.72**, favoring graphics accompanied by spoken rather than printed verbal information in the studied conditions. The effect varied with factors including pacing and element interactivity. [![Generated Image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://www.researchgate.net&sz=128) ResearchGate+1](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/223089224_Meta-analysis_of_the_modality_effect?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Reinwein's 2012 re-analysis substantially weakened the headline claim: across **86 effects**, the estimate fell to **d = 0.38**, and to approximately **d = 0.20** after a trim-and-fill publication-bias adjustment. A 2026 synthesis reports **N = 3,222** for the Reinwein corpus. [![Generated Image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov&sz=128) PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21989625/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Therefore:

> “Always narrate diagrams instead of using text”

is not a scientifically defensible protocol.

The stronger design question is:

> What must the learner inspect, retain, compare, revisit, or control at this moment?

Persistent printed text has advantages precisely because it does not disappear.

### 4.6 Generative processing is the target

The educational objective is not sensory stimulation.

The learner should construct relationships such as:

```
CAUSE
↓
MECHANISM
↓
CHANGE OF STATE
↓
CONSEQUENCE
```

A useful diagram externalizes relations that are difficult to infer from prose alone.

This helps explain why simplified diagrams can sometimes outperform visually richer ones.

Butcher's studies of cardiovascular learning compared text alone, text plus simplified diagrams, and text plus more detailed diagrams. Both diagram conditions supported mental-model development, while simplified diagrams were especially useful for integration and factual learning. [![Generated Image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://eric.ed.gov&sz=128) ERIC+1](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ734393&utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The relevant design property was not realism.

It was **representational usefulness for the required cognitive operation**.

## 5\. Fully Worked Clinical Example with Expert Think-Aloud

### Context

Year 8 science.

Learning objective:

> Explain why increasing temperature initially increases the rate of an enzyme-controlled reaction and why sufficiently high temperature subsequently decreases it.

The teacher originally planned to display a molecular animation beside a textbook paragraph.

Instead, the teacher uses a staged dual-representation sequence.

### Stage 1: activate the verbal causal structure

**Teacher:** “Complete this sentence: increasing temperature makes particles…”

**Student Maya:** “Move faster.”

**Teacher:** “Good. Does ‘move faster’ automatically mean enzymes work better forever?”

**Maya:** “Probably?”

**Teacher:** “Hold that prediction.”

**Clinical commentary:**  
The teacher does not immediately explain everything. The student's tentative model has been externalized and can now be tested.

### Stage 2: introduce one functional representation

The teacher displays a simple diagram:

```
LOWER TEMPERATURE

substrate       enzyme
   ●          [   ∪   ]

few effective collisions
```

Then:

```
MODERATE TEMPERATURE

 ● → → →      [ ∪ ]
     ●  →     [ ∪ ]

more successful collisions
```

**Teacher:** “Point to what changed.”

**Maya:** “The substrates are moving around more.”

**Teacher:** “What causal link should we add?”

**Maya:** “More movement means more collisions.”

Teacher writes:

> temperature ↑ → kinetic energy ↑ → collision frequency ↑

### Teacher think-aloud

> “I am deliberately leaving enzyme denaturation out for thirty seconds. Maya needs one causal chain stable before I introduce the competing high-temperature mechanism. If I animate binding, vibrate every molecule, label every bond, and explain denaturation simultaneously, I may create a beautiful simulation and a weak model.”

### Stage 3: reveal the inversion point

The teacher now displays:

```
EXCESSIVE TEMPERATURE

normal enzyme      altered enzyme

   [ ∪ ]      →       [ ~ ]

substrate fits        fit disrupted
```

**Teacher:** “Now apply your original rule: ‘higher temperature means faster reaction.’ What does it predict?”

**Maya:** “It should keep getting faster.”

**Teacher:** “What does this diagram add?”

**Maya:** “The enzyme changes shape.”

**Teacher:** “So which mechanism wins after a sufficiently high temperature?”

**Maya:** “The shape problem.”

**Teacher:** “Use the proper term.”

**Maya:** “Denaturation.”

### Stage 4: force verbal-pictorial integration

Teacher removes the labels.

**Teacher:** “Explain the entire graph using the diagram. No reading.”

Maya points to the ascending part of the rate-temperature curve.

**Maya:** “At first the particles have more kinetic energy, so collisions between enzyme and substrate happen more often.”

She points beyond the optimum.

**Maya:** “But at high temperature the enzyme's structure changes, so the active site doesn't bind the substrate properly, and the reaction rate falls.”

**Teacher:** “Why isn't ‘particles move faster’ enough to explain the whole graph?”

**Maya:** “Because two mechanisms are operating.”

### Clinical commentary

This exchange contains several expert moves:

1. **Prediction before explanation** exposes the learner's current causal model.
2. **Minimal representation** removes irrelevant perceptual structure.
3. **Temporal staging** avoids forcing the learner to coordinate both mechanisms immediately.
4. **Pointing and causal language** bind visual referents to propositions.
5. **Representation removal** tests whether the learner possesses the model rather than merely following the display.

### Stage 5: epistemic independence check

The student is now allowed to ask an AI tutor to produce another explanation.

The AI produces an elaborate molecular diagram.

**Teacher:** “Is the AI diagram evidence that you understand enzymes?”

**Maya:** “No.”

**Teacher:** “What would count?”

**Maya:** “I should be able to explain the curve without it and check whether its diagram matches the mechanism.”

**Teacher:** “Exactly. Find one feature of the AI explanation you can verify.”

Maya notices that the AI labels the optimum temperature as exactly 37°C.

**Maya:** “That's not universal. Different enzymes have different optima.”

This is the desired epistemic relation:

```
AI assistance
      ↓
representation / feedback / reduced production cost
      ↓
learner evaluates
      ↓
independent reconstruction
      ↓
transfer

NOT

AI produced correct artifact
      ↓
therefore learner understands
```

**Assistance Level != Competence Level.**

**Authorship != Mastery.**

## 6\. Non-Example / Pathological Case (Superficial Implementation)

A teacher learns about dual coding and establishes a rule:

> Every concept must contain words plus a picture.

The class is learning opportunity cost.

The teacher creates:

> **Opportunity cost:** the value of the next-best alternative forgone when a choice is made.

Beside it appears a decorative cartoon of a student holding money.

The teacher calls this “dual coding.”

It is not a useful implementation.

The cartoon represents **student + money**, while the difficult concept is the counterfactual relationship between:

- chosen option;
- rejected alternatives;
- ranking;
- value of the best rejected alternative.

A functionally designed representation might instead show:

```
AVAILABLE OPTIONS

A. Concert      value = 9
B. Restaurant   value = 7
C. Movie        value = 5

Learner chooses A

FORGONE:
B and C

OPPORTUNITY COST = value of B
                  = 7
```

Now the representation externalizes the conceptual structure.

### Lethal mutation 1: decorative imagery

The teacher asks:

> “What image could make this slide attractive?”

The expert asks:

> “What relationship is currently difficult to infer?”

Only the second question is tied to learning.

### Lethal mutation 2: icon substitution

Replacing every noun with an icon can make material harder to decode.

```
heart → ❤️
energy → ⚡
time → ⏱
money → 💰
```

This is visual rebus-making, not necessarily dual coding.

### Lethal mutation 3: media multiplication

```
text
+ same text narrated
+ same text captioned
+ animation
+ decorative photo
+ sound effects
```

The count of media rises while coherence falls.

### Lethal mutation 4: “visual learner” matching

**Teacher:** “Leo is a visual learner, so I'll teach him visually. Hana is auditory, so I'll narrate it to her.”

This confuses **multimedia learning theory** with the **learning-styles meshing hypothesis**.

Pashler, McDaniel, Rohrer, and Bjork's systematic review concluded that there was no adequate evidentiary basis for assigning instruction according to visual/auditory learning-style preferences. Preferences exist; that fact does not establish a preference × instructional-mode crossover showing superior learning from matched instruction. [![Generated Image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov&sz=128) PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26162104/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### Lethal mutation 5: outsourcing representation to AI

**Teacher:** “Ask the AI to create the diagram. If the diagram is correct, you're done.”

The artifact may be excellent while learner competence remains unknown.

A valid completion criterion is:

> Can the learner inspect, explain, correct, reconstruct, and transfer the underlying model after AI support is withdrawn?

## 8\. Formative Hinge Question & 4-Distractor Diagnostic Map

A teacher is explaining mitosis to novices. Students must later predict what would happen if sister chromatids failed to separate.

Which redesign is pedagogically best justified?

**A.** Add a decorative photograph of a microscope beside the existing paragraph because two codes are better than one.

**B.** Ask each student whether they is a visual or auditory learner; give diagrams to visual learners and spoken explanations to auditory learners.

**C.** Show a simplified sequence diagram that makes chromosome movement explicit, coordinate each stage with a concise explanation, then remove the explanation and ask students to reconstruct and predict from the sequence.

**D.** Show the simplified sequence while narrating a detailed paragraph and simultaneously display the identical paragraph as subtitles because three representations create stronger encoding.

### Diagnostic map

**A — Misconception: media-count model.**  
The learner believes that the mere addition of a pictorial code causes learning. They are not yet distinguishing **representational relevance** from decoration.

**B — Misconception: learning-styles meshing.**  
The learner has conflated dual-channel/dual-representation accounts with individual “visual versus auditory learner” taxonomies. Pashler et al.'s review found no adequate evidence supporting routine instructional matching by such preferences. [![Generated Image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov&sz=128) PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26162104/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

**C — Target model.**  
The learner understands that a simplified representation should externalize the causal/spatial process, that corresponding verbal and pictorial information should be coordinated, and that support must eventually be withdrawn to distinguish assisted processing from competence.

**D — Misconception: additive redundancy.**  
The learner correctly recognizes useful graphics but assumes duplicating the verbal stream necessarily provides additional encoding. They have not incorporated limited capacity, split attention, or redundancy effects.

### Teacher response routine

Do not announce “C is correct” immediately.

Ask:

**Teacher:** “A and D both increase multimedia. Why can one additional representation help while another hurts?”

A student who answers:

> “Because useful information has to be integrated, and duplicate or irrelevant information can consume processing without improving the mental model”

has crossed the conceptual hinge.

## 9\. Guided Rehearsal & Transfer Scenarios

### Near Transfer Scenario

You are redesigning a geography lesson explaining the water cycle.

Current slide:

- paragraph describing evaporation, condensation, precipitation, and runoff;
- unlabeled landscape photograph.

#### Teacher task

Replace the photograph with a schematic cycle.

Use four labels:

```
evaporation
     ↓
condensation
     ↓
precipitation
     ↓
runoff / collection
     ↺
```

Then coordinate language with the schematic.

**Teacher:** “Trace where the water is immediately before condensation.”

**Student:** “In the atmosphere as water vapour.”

**Teacher:** “Why does the arrow return to the starting system?”

**Student:** “Because it cycles rather than ending at runoff.”

Now remove the four process labels.

**Teacher:** “Reconstruct them.”

Then change the problem:

> “Suppose surface temperature rises while all else is initially equal. Which transition is most directly affected first?”

The sequence moves from:

```
representation recognition
→ verbal-pictorial integration
→ reconstruction
→ causal transfer
```

The teacher should not judge success from whether students can recite the four nouns while arrows remain labelled.

### Far Transfer Scenario

A software engineering instructor teaches how HTTP requests pass through an authenticated API.

The content is abstract rather than inherently pictorial.

Prose alone:

> The client sends a request. Middleware validates authentication. Authorized requests reach a controller, which calls the service layer. The service accesses the database and returns a result.

A useful representation is:

```
Client
  |
  v
Auth middleware
  |
  | valid
  v
Controller
  |
  v
Service
  |
  v
Database

invalid auth
     |
     └──> 401
```

Now use diagnosis.

**Instructor:** “Where should an invalid request stop?”

**Student:** “Middleware.”

**Instructor:** “If the database log shows a query from an invalid token, which invariant was violated?”

**Student:** “The request crossed the authentication boundary.”

The diagram contributes because the target knowledge contains:

- sequence;
- dependency;
- branching;
- boundary;
- permitted and forbidden transitions.

The principle transfers far beyond textbook illustration.

### AI-mediated far transfer

Student asks an AI system:

> “Draw the architecture for me.”

The AI creates the diagram correctly.

That is evidence of **tool output quality**.

It is not evidence of the student's architectural model.

The instructor therefore changes the state:

```
AI ON:
student inspects/generated architecture

AI OFF:
student reconstructs trust boundary

PERTURBATION:
"Move auth after the controller. What failure becomes possible?"

TRANSFER:
student predicts consequence
```

A productive AI tutor thus reduces representational production costs while preserving epistemic checks.

## 10\. Turn-Key Operational Protocol

Use the following **MODEL–BIND–FADE–PROBE** protocol whenever considering multimedia instruction.

### State 1 — MODEL the learning relation

Before selecting media, complete:

> “After instruction, the learner must understand that ______ relates to ______ because ______.”

Examples:

> “Pressure decreases when chamber volume increases because…”

> “Opportunity cost refers to the highest-valued rejected alternative because…”

If the teacher cannot identify the relation, media selection is premature.

### State 2 — Choose the minimum functional representation

Ask:

1. Is the difficult information **spatial**?
2. Is it **temporal**?
3. Is it **causal**?
4. Is it **quantitative or relational**?
5. Would an external representation reduce unnecessary mental search?

Possible mappings:

| Knowledge structure | Candidate representation |
| --- | --- |
| spatial anatomy | labelled schematic |
| causal process | causal diagram |
| temporal change | segmented animation or sequence |
| comparison | aligned table |
| quantitative relationship | graph |
| hierarchy | tree |
| system flow | node-edge diagram |

Do not add an image simply to satisfy the protocol.

### State 3 — BIND words to referents

Coordinate corresponding information.

Instead of:

**Teacher:** “As explained in the paragraph on the left, several mechanisms contribute here.”

Use:

**Teacher:** “This arrow shows the pressure drop. When pressure falls below atmospheric pressure, this valve opens.”

Point while saying “this arrow” and “this valve.”

For static instructional materials, place explanatory language close to its referent when practical.

### State 4 — Remove processing competitors

Run four checks:

**Coherence check**

> Does every element contribute to the target mental model?

**Redundancy check**

> Is identical information being presented in multiple forms without a reason?

**Attention check**

> Must the learner visually search between sources that could be integrated?

**Transience check**

> Does essential information disappear before learners can compare it?

A narration-plus-animation presentation may benefit from:

- pause;
- replay;
- segmentation;
- signalling;
- key-term labels.

### State 5 — Elicit active integration

Do not ask only:

> “Does that make sense?”

Ask:

> “Point to the feature that corresponds to this sentence.”

> “Explain what this arrow means.”

> “Which part of the diagram supports your claim?”

> “What would change if this component disappeared?”

These prompts make integration observable.

### State 6 — FADE the representation

Reduce assistance.

Sequence:

```
full representation + teacher explanation
↓
representation + learner explanation
↓
partial representation
↓
blank reconstruction
↓
novel problem
```

This is especially important with AI.

When an AI tutor supplied the diagram or explanation, include at least one support-withdrawal probe before declaring mastery.

### State 7 — PROBE transfer

Change one structural property.

Examples:

**Physics:**  
“What happens if resistance doubles?”

**Biology:**  
“What happens if this valve fails?”

**Economics:**  
“What if the second-best alternative changes?”

**Software:**  
“What if authentication occurs after database access?”

A student who memorized labels will often fail here.

A student with an integrated model can reason.

### State 8 — Feedback by representation

Avoid:

> “Wrong.”

Use:

**Teacher:** “Your verbal explanation says blood returns to the left atrium, but your arrow enters the right atrium. Which representation do you trust, and how can we resolve the conflict?”

This turns conflicting representations into diagnostic evidence.

### State 9 — Close with epistemic verification

Ask:

> “Which parts of this representation are evidence, which are explanatory simplifications, and which assumptions would you check?”

For AI-generated material add:

> “What did the AI contribute, what did you verify, and what can you now reproduce independently?”

The completed protocol therefore follows the DESIGN:OS authoring formula:

```
Knowledge
→ Experience
→ Diagnosis
→ Practice
→ Feedback
→ Transfer
→ Protocol
```

## 11\. Empirical Evidence, Effect Sizes & Boundary Conditions

### Empirical Studies

#### 1\. Mayer & Anderson (1991): temporal coordination

Mayer and Anderson tested mechanically inexperienced college students learning a bicycle-pump mechanism.

**N = 102** across two experiments. [![Generated Image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://eric.ed.gov&sz=128) ERIC](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ442338&utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Presenting corresponding narration and animation together produced substantially better problem-solving transfer than separating them; reported temporal-contiguity effects were approximately **d = 1.00 and d = 1.05**. [![Generated Image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://iep.jsu.edu&sz=128) Jax State](https://iep.jsu.edu/online/faculty/MULTIMEDIA%20LEARNING%20by%20Richard%20E.%20Mayer.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

**Pedagogical inference:** coordination can facilitate referential connections between representations.

**What the study does not prove:** every animation needs narration, every lesson should use animation, or two modalities automatically increase learning.

* * *

#### 2\. Mayer & Anderson (1992): concurrent representation and transfer

Two experiments involved **N = 280 college students** studying bicycle-pump or automotive-braking mechanisms. [![Generated Image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://eric.ed.gov&sz=128) ERIC](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ456638&utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Concurrent animation and narration produced stronger problem-solving performance than several successive or single-medium conditions. Temporal-contiguity transfer effects were approximately **d = 1.61 and d = 1.33** for the two experiments. [![Generated Image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://iep.jsu.edu&sz=128) Jax State](https://iep.jsu.edu/online/faculty/MULTIMEDIA%20LEARNING%20by%20Richard%20E.%20Mayer.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Retention differences were much less diagnostic than transfer differences, reinforcing an important distinction:

> remembering propositions and constructing an operational mental model are not identical outcomes.

* * *

#### 3\. Multimedia-principle experimental corpus

Mayer's synthesis of 11 experimental comparisons reported that words-plus-pictures conditions outperformed words-alone conditions on transfer in all 11 comparisons, with a median effect of approximately **d = 1.39**. [![Generated Image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://www.cambridge.org&sz=128) Cambridge University Press+1](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/multimedia-learning/multimedia-principle/1CC3DE892B0431BA48B4C4DCA10D0B8F?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

A later handbook summary of 13 tests reported a median **d = 1.35**. [![Generated Image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://www.cambridge.org&sz=128) Cambridge University Press](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-handbook-of-multimedia-learning/multimedia-principle/D09A773C7C5C214FA19D4C9841FBC83B?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

These unusually large classic effects should not be treated as a universal expected impact of multimedia instruction. Much of this evidence came from tightly controlled experimental materials designed by a research group specializing in these principles.

* * *

#### 4\. Guo, Zhang, Wright & McTigue (2020): broader graphics evidence

This meta-analysis synthesized:

- **39 experimental studies**
- **N = 2,103**
- 1,053 learners in experimental conditions
- 1,050 in control conditions.

The random-effects estimate for graphics plus text compared with text alone was:

> **Hedges' g = 0.39, SE = 0.06, 95% CI [0.26, 0.51], p < .001.**

Individual study effects ranged from **g = -0.23 to 1.24**. [![Generated Image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://journals.sagepub.com&sz=128) Sage Journals+1](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/2332858420901696?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

That distribution supplies a crucial correction to simplistic dual-coding advocacy.

Graphics are not uniformly beneficial.

The design, learner, task, assessment, and relation between text and graphic matter.

* * *

#### 5\. Butcher (2006): representational simplicity

Butcher examined cardiovascular-system learning using:

- text only;
- text with simplified diagrams;
- text with more detailed diagrams.

Both diagram conditions could support mental-model development, while simplified diagrams particularly supported integration and factual learning. [![Generated Image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://eric.ed.gov&sz=128) ERIC+1](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ734393&utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The Guo meta-analysis codes one Butcher experiment comparison with **n = 11 experimental and n = 11 control**, producing **g = 0.33, 95% CI [-0.51, 1.17]** for that contrast. [![Generated Image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://journals.sagepub.com&sz=128) Sage Journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/2332858420901696?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The wide interval is a reminder that individual small experiments provide noisy estimates even when theoretically informative.

* * *

#### 6\. Ginns (2005): modality effect

Ginns synthesized **43 independent effects**, including 39 between-subject comparisons.

The weighted between-subject estimate was:

> **d = 0.72, 95% CI [0.52, 0.92].**

The effect was heterogeneous and moderated by properties including element interactivity and pacing. [![Generated Image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://www.researchgate.net&sz=128) ResearchGate+1](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/223089224_Meta-analysis_of_the_modality_effect?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

This supports a conditional advantage for distributing processing across spoken language and graphics in some multimedia environments.

It does not warrant a universal narration mandate.

* * *

#### 7\. Reinwein (2012): adversarial re-analysis of modality

Reinwein replicated and broadened the modality meta-analysis using **86 independent effects**.

The estimated advantage fell to:

> **d = 0.38**

and to roughly:

> **d = 0.20**

after trim-and-fill correction for possible publication bias. [![Generated Image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov&sz=128) PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21989625/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

A 2026 meta-analytic evidence index reports **N = 3,222** across the relevant Reinwein studies. [![Generated Image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://www.visiblelearningmetax.com&sz=128) Visible Learning MetaX](https://www.visiblelearningmetax.com/influences/view/modality_effect?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

This adversarial evidence is pedagogically valuable because it changes the appropriate claim from:

> “The modality principle is strongly established.”

to:

> “A modality advantage occurs under identifiable conditions, but its average magnitude and theoretical explanation are less secure than early demonstrations suggested.”

* * *

#### 8\. Cromley & Chen (2025): modern boundary-condition evidence

A 2025 multilevel meta-analysis examined Mayer's multimedia research corpus:

- **92 articles**
- **181 studies**
- **591 effect sizes**.

The overall effect across the corpus was **Hedges' g = 0.37**, with substantial moderation. Effects differed by design principle, medium, outcome, domain, age, year, and other characteristics. [![Generated Image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://www.sciencedirect.com&sz=128) ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1747938X25000673?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Reported estimates included approximately:

- removing seductive details: **g = 1.00**;
- modality principle: **g = 0.82** for the coded comparison;
- personalization: **g = 0.70**;
- multimedia principle: **g = 0.68** under one moderator specification;
- sentence-level coherence: **g = 0.63**;
- self-explanation: **g = 0.46**;
- cueing: **g = 0.24**. [![Generated Image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://www.sciencedirect.com&sz=128) ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1747938X25000673?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Several principles did not show significant average effects in that analysis.

The investigators also observed smaller contemporary estimates than some earlier headline effects.

That is precisely why pedagogical doctrine should be built around **mechanisms and boundary conditions**, rather than memorized effect-size slogans.

* * *

### Adversarial Debate

#### Claim: “Dual coding has overwhelming empirical support because words plus pictures produce effects above d = 1.”

**Challenge:** Classic Mayer experiments often found very large effects, but broader meta-analysis estimates are much smaller. Guo et al. estimate **g = 0.39** across 39 experiments, while Cromley and Chen's broader multimedia corpus yields an overall **g = 0.37**. [![Generated Image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://journals.sagepub.com&sz=128) Sage Journals+1](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/2332858420901696?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

**Resolution:** The existence of a multimedia advantage is credible; a universal effect near one standard deviation is not.

* * *

#### Claim: “Dual coding proves visual and auditory learners need different teaching.”

**Challenge:** This inference does not follow from Dual Coding Theory or CTML. The learning-styles meshing hypothesis requires a specific crossover interaction whereby one instructional treatment benefits one learner type and another treatment benefits the other. Pashler et al. found essentially no convincing evidence supporting educational use of this matching practice. [![Generated Image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov&sz=128) PubMed+1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26162104/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

**Resolution:** Representation choice should follow the structure of knowledge and processing constraints rather than learner-style labels.

* * *

#### Claim: “Two channels double working-memory capacity.”

**Challenge:** Neither Dual Coding Theory nor contemporary multimedia-learning evidence justifies treating cognition as two independent storage tanks whose capacities simply add.

The systems interact. Visual text and graphics may compete. Spoken language disappears over time. Representations require coordination. Knowledge changes processing demands.

**Resolution:** Talk about **partly differentiated processing resources**, not a literal doubling of memory.

* * *

#### Claim: “Pictures create a second memory trace, so any picture improves recall.”

**Challenge:** Graphics can be irrelevant, misleading, overly detailed, difficult to map to text, or cognitively expensive. Individual effects in the Guo meta-analysis ranged into negative territory. [![Generated Image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://journals.sagepub.com&sz=128) Sage Journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/2332858420901696?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

**Resolution:** Useful representations must encode relationships relevant to the target mental model.

* * *

#### Claim: “AI can implement dual coding automatically.”

**Challenge:** Generative systems can rapidly create illustrations, diagrams, animations, summaries, labels, and narration, but generation quality and learner cognition are different variables.

An AI may:

```
compress explanation
draw diagram
label relations
animate mechanism
answer questions
```

while the learner performs:

```
almost no selection
almost no organization
almost no reconstruction
almost no verification
```

**Resolution:** AI should carry production and search costs where useful while preserving learner prediction, integration, critique, reconstruction, and transfer.

### Boundary Conditions & Inversion Points

#### 1\. Prior knowledge

Novices often benefit more from explicit representational guidance because they lack schemas for organizing information.

Experts may find the same material redundant.

The multimedia principle itself appears stronger in some low-knowledge populations than in high-knowledge populations. [![Generated Image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://www.cambridge.org&sz=128) Cambridge University Press](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/multimedia-learning/multimedia-principle/1CC3DE892B0431BA48B4C4DCA10D0B8F?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

This produces an **expertise inversion**:

```
NOVICE:
guidance → useful

EXPERT:
same guidance → unnecessary processing
```

* * *

#### 2\. Representational relevance

A graphic helps only to the extent that it makes useful structure more available.

Decorative photographs, anthropomorphic mascots, irrelevant stories, and seductive animations can consume attention without improving the target model.

The question is not:

> “Is there an image?”

It is:

> “What inference becomes easier because this representation exists?”

* * *

#### 3\. Visual complexity

Narration may reduce visual competition when learners must inspect a complex diagram.

But adding spoken language can create new problems when:

- learners need exact terminology;
- content must be reread;
- pronunciation is unfamiliar;
- narration is rapid;
- learners are using a second language;
- the audio is inaccessible.

Persistent text can therefore outperform transient narration under some conditions.

* * *

#### 4\. Pace and learner control

System-paced animations create transient-information demands.

Learner-controlled:

- pause;
- rewind;
- segmentation;
- replay;

can change the processing problem substantially.

A modality recommendation established with system-paced animation should not automatically be generalized to a static, self-paced textbook.

* * *

#### 5\. Accessibility

A pedagogical rule cannot override access.

Learners who are deaf or hard of hearing need textual equivalents for narration.

Learners who are blind or have low vision may need verbal descriptions of graphical information.

The correct design problem becomes:

> How can equivalent conceptual relationships be made available through accessible representations without introducing unnecessary duplication for each learner?

Accessibility is part of instructional architecture, not an exception to it.

* * *

#### 6\. Intrinsic task structure

Some knowledge is naturally spatial:

- anatomy;
- geometry;
- circuitry;
- geography.

Some is relational:

- causality;
- hierarchy;
- system architecture.

Some propositions gain little from pictorial representation.

Forcing all content into pictures can increase decoding demands.

Dual coding therefore provides a **design opportunity**, not a requirement that every proposition receive an illustration.

* * *

#### 7\. Animation versus static representation

Animation is particularly defensible when change itself is the object of learning.

Even then, transient information can make comparison difficult.

A static sequence may sometimes outperform continuous motion because the learner can compare states side by side.

Choose animation for an instructional reason, not because reality moves.

* * *

#### 8\. Redundancy

Redundancy becomes harmful when duplicate information consumes capacity needed for model construction.

But apparent redundancy can serve legitimate functions:

- accessibility;
- second-language learning;
- unfamiliar technical vocabulary;
- noisy environments;
- learner choice;
- later reference.

The principle is therefore conditional:

> Remove cognitively unnecessary redundancy while preserving redundancy serving an identified learning or access function.

* * *

#### 9\. Assessment level

Multimedia may affect:

```
recognition
≠
recall
≠
explanation
≠
transfer
```

A lesson that raises immediate recall without improving transfer has achieved a different result from one that enables causal reasoning.

Measure the outcome the lesson claims to produce.

* * *

#### 10\. AI assistance inversion

AI creates a new boundary condition absent from classic multimedia experiments.

As assistance rises:

```
production effort ↓
search effort ↓
drawing effort ↓
explanation effort ↓
```

but learner generative processing may also fall.

Therefore DESIGN:OS treats AI-supported multimedia learning as a two-axis system:

```
ASSISTANCE LEVEL
AI generates / explains / annotates / checks

            independent from

COMPETENCE LEVEL
learner reconstructs / predicts / critiques / transfers
```

A high-assistance learner may have high competence.

A high-assistance learner may also have near-zero competence.

The artifact cannot distinguish those states.

The teacher must.

The operational rule is therefore:

> **Use multimedia to externalize useful relationships; use instruction to make learners coordinate those relationships; use fading and transfer to establish that the resulting model belongs to the learner.**

That formulation preserves the strongest empirical insight of dual coding and multimedia learning while rejecting its most common neuromyths: humans are not fixed visual or auditory learners, additional media do not monotonically improve learning, realism is not equivalent to instructional quality, and an AI-generated multimodal explanation is not evidence of human mastery.

# Relative utility of ten study techniques

The abstract reviews ten techniques against learning conditions, student characteristics, materials and criterion tasks. Practice testing and distributed practice received high utility ratings. Elaborative interrogation, self-explanation and interleaved practice received moderate ratings. Summarization, highlighting, the keyword mnemonic, imagery for text learning and rereading received low ratings. The authors state that most students report rereading and highlighting, that these do not consistently boost performance, and that other techniques should be used in their place, for example practice testing instead of rereading.

**Design use:** When a learner's plan is to reread, offer closed-book retrieval and spaced revisits as the main repeat activity.

**Scope read:** Official abstract only (Europe PMC mirror of the PubMed record), accessed 2026-10-29. The per-technique reviews and their boundary conditions were not read. Utility ratings are the authors' qualitative judgments, not effect sizes; they are not converted into grades or months of progress.

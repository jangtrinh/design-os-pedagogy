# Underestimating one's own completion time

The abstract reports that participants' predictions of their own completion times were too optimistic for a variety of academic and nonacademic tasks. Think-aloud data showed a focus on future plan scenarios rather than relevant past experiences. In Study 4 the optimistic bias was eliminated for participants instructed to connect relevant past experiences with their predictions. Participants attributed past prediction failures to external, transient and specific factors, and observers overestimated others' completion times.

**Design use:** Ask a learner to estimate a study budget from comparable past tasks, not only from a plan, and treat an overrun as a forecasting error to correct.

Participant population, sample sizes and the size of the bias were not extracted. Not tested on long skill-learning budgets; applying it to them is an authored extrapolation.

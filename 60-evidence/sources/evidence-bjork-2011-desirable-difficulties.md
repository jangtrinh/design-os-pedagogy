# Desirable difficulties and the learning-performance distinction

**Extracted (pp. 56-58):** the chapter distinguishes performance during study from
durable learning, and argues that current performance can be a highly unreliable
index of learning. Rereading can produce a feeling of fluency that learners
mistake for understanding. Conditions that speed apparent progress often fail to
support long-term retention and transfer, whereas conditions that create
challenge and slow apparent progress often improve them. The named examples of
desirable difficulties are varying practice conditions, interleaving, spacing, and
using tests instead of re-presentation.

**Boundary stated by the authors (p. 58):** a difficulty is desirable only when the
learner has the background knowledge or skill to respond to it successfully;
otherwise it becomes an undesirable difficulty.

**Scope read:** pp. 55-58 only. Specific drills outside the authors' named list
(for example disabling editor autocomplete) are not discussed there; classifying
them as desirable difficulties is an authored inference that needs its own test.

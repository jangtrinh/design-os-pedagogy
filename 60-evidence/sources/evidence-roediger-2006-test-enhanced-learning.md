# Roediger & Karpicke (2006): testing versus restudying prose

## What the abstract states (read 2026-10-29)

- Two experiments. Students studied prose passages, then either took one or
  three immediate free-recall tests without feedback, or restudied the material
  the same number of times.
- A final retention test came 5 minutes, 2 days or 1 week later.
- At 5 minutes, repeated studying gave better recall than repeated testing. On
  the delayed tests, prior testing gave greater retention than studying.
- Repeated studying increased students' confidence in remembering the material.

## Use in this repository

Supports the design choice that closure and review should use a closed-book
recall attempt rather than another reading pass, and that learner confidence
after rereading is not evidence of delayed retention. The participant population,
sample sizes and effect estimates were not extracted; do not quote numbers from
this record beyond those listed above. No Grade is assigned until appraisal.

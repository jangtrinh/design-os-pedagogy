# Far transfer from chess, music and working-memory training

The abstract reports two meta-analyses of chess and music instruction for children and a third of working-memory training, each on cognitive and academic outcomes. Effects were small to moderate, but effect sizes were inversely related to the quality of the experimental design, such as the presence of active control groups. The authors conclude that far transfer of learning rarely occurs.

**Design use:** Treat claims that one activity improves general thinking as hypotheses that need active-control evidence on the outcome of interest.

**Scope read:** Official abstract only, accessed 2026-10-29. Study counts and estimates were not read. Programming instruction is not one of the three trained activities; extending this result to programming is an inference to check against programming-specific evidence.

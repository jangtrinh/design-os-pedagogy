# Relevance writing and interest in high-school science

The abstract describes a randomized field experiment with high-school students. A relevance intervention asked students to connect what they were learning in science to their own lives. It increased interest in science and course grades for students with low expectations of success; the hypothesis was that the effect would be stronger for that group.

**Design use:** When a learner says a topic is useless to them, prompting them to write their own connections is a testable alternative to telling them to ignore usefulness.

**Scope read:** Official abstract only (Europe PMC mirror of the PubMed record), accessed 2026-10-29. Sample size, effect estimates, measures, timing and the effect for higher-expectation students were not read. No effect size is asserted. The result is from one study in high-school science; it is not evidence for every age, subject or delivery mode.

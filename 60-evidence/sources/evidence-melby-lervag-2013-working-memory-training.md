# Working-memory training and transfer

The abstract reports 23 randomized or quasi-experimental studies with 30 group comparisons, covering clinical samples and typically developing children and adults. Training produced reliable short-term gains in working-memory skills. Verbal working-memory gains were not sustained at follow-up; limited evidence suggested visuospatial gains might be. There was no convincing evidence of generalization to nonverbal or verbal ability, inhibitory processes in attention, word decoding or arithmetic. The authors conclude that the programs produce short-term, specific effects that do not generalize.

**Design use:** Do not sell a general "memory workout" as a route to better subject learning. Build the specific knowledge and retrieval the target task needs.

The review concerns working-memory training programs; it does not test mnemonic strategies, retrieval practice or domain-knowledge building.

# Transfer from learning to program

The abstract reports a three-level random-effects meta-analysis of 105 studies and 539 effect sizes. It found a moderate overall transfer effect (g = 0.49, 95% CI [0.37, 0.61]), a strong near-transfer effect (g = 0.75, 95% CI [0.39, 1.11]) and a moderate far-transfer effect (g = 0.47, 95% CI [0.35, 0.59]). Positive transfer appeared for creative thinking, mathematical skills and metacognition, followed by spatial skills and reasoning. School achievement and literacy benefited least. Effects were significantly larger in studies with untreated controls than with active controls, and published studies showed larger effects than gray literature.

**Design use:** Programming can be justified by programming outcomes and by some measured cognitive outcomes. A claim that it trains general life thinking goes beyond the outcomes measured here.

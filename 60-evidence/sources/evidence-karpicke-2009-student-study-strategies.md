# What students do when they study alone

The abstract reports a survey of 177 college students with a free-report question about study strategies and a forced choice between rereading and practising recall after studying a chapter. Both questions pointed the same way: most students repeatedly read notes or textbooks, and relatively few self-test. The authors propose that many students experience illusions of competence while studying and that these illusions affect which strategies they choose.

**Design use:** A learner's confidence after rereading is a self-report to check with a closed-book recall probe, not evidence of retention.

**Scope read:** Official abstract only (Europe PMC mirror of the PubMed record), accessed 2026-10-29. The survey is descriptive; the illusion-of-competence account is the authors' proposed explanation, not a tested result of this study. Exact percentages were not read.

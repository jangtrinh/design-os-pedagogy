# Deliberate practice explains part, not all, of performance variance

**Extracted from the abstract:** the authors tested the view that individual
differences in performance largely reflect differences in the amount of
deliberate practice. Across the domains in which deliberate practice had been
studied, it explained 26% of the variance in performance for games, 21% for
music, 18% for sports, 4% for education and less than 1% for professions. The
abstract concludes that deliberate practice is important but less important than
had been argued.

**Metric note:** these are proportions of variance explained, not Cohen's d,
Hedges' g or months of progress. Do not convert or rank them against effect sizes
elsewhere in this library.

**Scope read:** abstract only. How studies defined and measured deliberate practice
and how the domain estimates were computed were
not checked. Published replies or later reanalyses were not searched in this run.

**Design use:** reject both extremes. Practice design matters and is the part an
educator controls; the evidence does not support telling learners that ability
differences are irrelevant.

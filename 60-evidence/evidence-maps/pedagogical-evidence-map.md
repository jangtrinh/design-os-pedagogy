# Pedagogical Evidence Map

This is an authored planning aid. The previous per-stage effect-size cells lacked
source locators and have been removed. Unknown applicability remains unknown.

## Canonical stages

| Code | Educational setting or role | Information to collect separately |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| S0 | Prenatal and caregiver readiness | Caregiver needs and educational-support boundaries |
| S1 | Early childhood, typically ages 0–6 | Development, language, play and access needs |
| S2 | Primary education, typically ages 6–11 | Prior knowledge and task-specific performance |
| S3 | Secondary education, typically ages 11–18 | Prior knowledge, task and learning context |
| S4 | Tertiary education | Prior knowledge in this subject, not presumed expertise |
| S5 | Postgraduate and doctoral research | Research task and disciplinary experience |
| S6 | Adult and lifelong learning | Goals and new-domain novice status |
| S7 | Educator development and teacher coaching | Teaching task and evidence of practice |

Stages describe settings or roles, not a ladder of cognitive expertise. Use
AL0–AL7 for AI assistance and record domain proficiency separately. An adult
learning programming can need a worked example. Age alone is not a reason to
withhold instruction, prescribe a method or declare an intervention harmful.

## Decision protocol

1. Record the goal, context, access needs and prior-knowledge evidence.
2. Treat an error as a signal. Collect reasoning or a contrasting item before
   selecting among misconceptions, procedural gaps and isolated slips.
3. Inspect a bounded claim, its source and review status. A legacy Grade A label
   is not an appraisal. Unreviewed guidance cannot establish an effect prediction.
4. Choose an explanation, worked example, representation, questioning sequence
   or practice task and record why it fits the observed need.
5. Check reasoning on a new independent item; adapt assistance using evidence.
   One correct answer does not change life stage or prove durable mastery.
   Include a delayed transfer check where feasible.

## Worked decision

An adult engineer learning a new programming concept cannot explain a failed
example. A scaffolded worked example followed by self-explanation is one
reasonable option. A contrasting trace or guided question may also fit. Record
the learner's response and next independent check rather than selecting an S6
numeric effect-size cell.

## Boundaries

This map supplies no universal harm cutoff, per-stage treatment effect or
transfer guarantee. Estimates need a population, comparator, outcome, timepoint
and exact source locator. A population average cannot be stacked with other
effects or applied directly to an individual. Missing evidence remains visible.
